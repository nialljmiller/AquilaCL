This plan was written on 2026-11-25. Nothing in it has been started. The code for steps 3–5 exists, has been tested only on synthetic data, and has not been run on the real data.

## Corrections to what has been reported so far

1. **"Every model finds the same six states" is partly an artefact of the search range.**
   - The search for K starts at 6 (`states.py`, `k_min=6`).
   - No run met the stability rule, and the fallback picked the most stable K within 6–30, which was 6 in every run.
   - Smaller K was never tried, and states were never matched across runs.
2. **"Identity was reduced without losing behaviour" was not testable as measured.**
   - Every behaviour probe stays the same under a yaw rotation: VeDBA is a vector length, and speed, climb, turn and the labels all come from GPS.
   - The ±60° augmentation is about 4 times the measured spread of mounting yaw between birds (SD 15.8°).
3. **Window shares are not time budgets.**
   - Continuous mode, which records ACC and GPS every second, is almost always flight (25 of 91,913 windows are still).
   - One flight can therefore produce hundreds of windows, while a scheduled burst produces one.
   - The flapping state is 18% of flight windows. Published golden eagle data put flapping at about 2% of flight time.
4. **The 12 held-out birds were used to choose among six runs**, so they are no longer an untouched test set.

## What is sound

- Ingest checks.
- The per-burst-type diagnosis of sensitivity and the common amplitude format.
- The re-quantisation counterfactual.
- Bird-level statistics and nuisance probes against a handcrafted floor.
- Solar time.
- Superseded results are marked as such.

The pooled flight speeds of the states match published golden eagle values:
- thermal circling: about 10 m/s, climbing +1.4 m/s (published: 10.2 m/s, +1.76 m/s);
- inter-thermal glide: about 18 m/s, sinking −1.9 m/s (published: 17.6–18.1 m/s);
- slope soaring: about 11 m/s (published: 10.9 m/s).

## Verified since the reviews

- Scheduled (non-continuous) bursts start on a fixed clock for most birds:
  - most common interval 300 s (81 birds), 900 s (10 birds), 3600 s (1 bird);
  - per bird, a median 64% of intervals equal that value.
- These bursts are therefore close to a behaviour-independent sample. Before they can be used for time budgets, the cause of the irregular third of intervals must be checked.

## Questions only the owner can answer

1. **Recording triggers.** What starts continuous recording: speed, flight, time of day, battery, or a remote command? This decides how the time budgets in step 1 are built.
2. **Age at tagging.** Do any birds have an independently known age beyond the comment field ("nestling", "fledgling", "HY", "chick")? For example, plumage notes from capture.
3. **Individual differences.** Are they a signal to keep (individual flight style, demographic differences) or a nuisance to remove? Proposal: keep them for demography and anomaly work, and remove them only for state discovery.
4. **Ground truth.** Are there any observations that could serve as an independent check: video, field observations, or known nest-attendance periods?
